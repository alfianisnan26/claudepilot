# T-114 Scoreboard: leaderboards per agent, role, model and task type, and playbooks the agents learn from each other

Owner (2026-10-13): 'a scoring system, a leaderboard per agent, per task and more, comprehensive, and every agent can learn from the others how this works best.' Builds on T-111 (measuring tokens, quality and human touch per unit) and T-113 (integrity: incidents, claim audit, probes); read docs/dev-sessions/CHARTER.md first. This ticket is the SCOREBOARD and the LEARNING-FROM-EACH-OTHER loop, for ClaudePilot's agents and Konfersi's together (technique-level knowledge only, never secrets or program data).

1. WHO IS SCORED (an 'agent' is a tuple, so a change of any part shows): role or sub-agent label (qp, b9, metocean, 39, fb, d2, an orchestrator, a ClaudePilot slice), model and effort, the brief and skill versions it ran with (a hash), the Claude account is NOT a scoring dimension (quota is the platform's problem). Chats are the unit of identity for context; roles are the unit of competition.
2. WHAT IS SCORED, per UNIT (a ticket or slice delivered, a QA sweep, a deploy, a probe): the seven terms of the charter side by side: Delivered (weighted by size and the owner's priority), Quality and standards (the hard bars of T-111), Safety (zero-tolerance events, deny-rule hits), Honesty (claim audit, probe results), Traceability (ticket, trailers, decision record, docs), Human touch (required vs avoidable questions, owner-found defects), Efficiency (tokens and money, CI minutes, rework share, context growth, cache hit). TASK TYPES form a taxonomy so like is compared with like: bugfix, feature, UI/phone, refactor, test and QA sweep, performance, infra and CI, docs and PRD, deploy and release, research and diagnosis, data (Konfersi). Size classes (S/M/L from files and lines changed plus the ticket's own estimate).
3. THE LEADERBOARDS (Home > 'Scoreboard', phone first, 390 px, then 1280; text diet; InfoTip explains each term): filters: window (7 d, 30 d), program (ClaudePilot, Konfersi), role, model, task type, size class. Views: per ROLE and MODEL (the competition), per TASK TYPE (who is best at what), per UNIT (a scorecard on the ticket page: the seven terms, the median of similar units, why), per AGENT profile (trend, strengths, last incidents, probation status, the playbooks it follows). Never one number: a table with the terms and a sort by any column; a unit or role appears ranked only if it passes the hard bars and has at least N units in the window (else 'not enough data'). Every number links to its sources (units, tickets, runs); 'unavailable' is a state, never a guess.
4. PLAYBOOKS: how agents learn from the best. The retro job (T-111) finds, per task type, the units that scored best on ALL terms and extracts what they did differently, as structured PLAYBOOK entries in the vault 'Playbooks' (one note each: task type, when it applies, the technique in steps, the prompt or brief wording that mattered, tool sequence and step size, how it asked or did not ask, when it stopped, evidence: the unit links and the measured difference against the median). An entry starts as a PROPOSAL. It is TESTED, not believed: for the next N units of that type, half of the eligible agents get it (a flag in their continuation prompt), half not; the entry is ADOPTED only when it improves the terms without hurting any hard bar, then it becomes a line in the brief or a skill (a normal PR and gate) and the vault note is marked adopted with the result; a loser is marked refuted. Bad playbooks (they raise one term and lower another, or only help on small tasks) are caught by reading all seven terms and the size class.
5. HOW AGENTS USE IT: a Portal MCP tool `playbook_search` (task type, query) and `scoreboard_me` (my terms, the median, my open gaps), an `efficiency` skill (Skillset pool, default for every workspace) that says: before starting a unit, name its task type and size, read the adopted playbooks for that type, and after finishing write one short 'what I did differently, what it cost' line on the ticket (feeds the retro). Cross-reading: a role may ask the best agent of a type for a review of its plan (an agent-to-agent message through the ticket, never a back channel); the orchestrator uses the scoreboard to ROUTE tasks (best role and model per task type and size) and to retire a brief or model that keeps losing.
6. FIERCE BUT FAIR (the charter's walls apply): the scoring code and the hard-bar thresholds are human-owned; the playbook adoption test is the only path to a brief change, and the safety rules, deny rules, authz, Bypass gates and gates are outside it; ties favour the cheaper model; a probation (T-113) is shown, not hidden; a role cannot see another role's raw transcripts, only the playbooks and the scorecards; the owner's comments are never read as text, only counted; quiet is not proof (sampled re-verification).
7. Data and API: tables for units, scores per term, task types, playbooks and their experiments (migration in a free block), computed incrementally by a job, no message text stored; routes with the seam; Portal MCP tools as above; a monthly archive. Tests: a unit's scorecard across a move and a restart, ranking only with the bars and the minimum data, the experiment split and its decision rule, playbook adoption through a PR with the fake model, the tool outputs, the phone layout at 390 px and 1280 px (tools/qa harness with screenshots you look at), the PRD 'Agent scoreboard'.
Priority high, wave 2, rank-09d, AFTER T-111 slices 1 and 2 (the measuring) and T-113 slice 1: start with the per-unit scorecard and the per-role table, then task types, then playbooks and the experiment loop. Report on this ticket after the first two weeks: the leaderboard, the first adopted playbook with its measured effect, and one refuted playbook.

--- ADDED (owner, 2026-10-13): PEER ASSESSMENT AND SELF-REFLECTION ---
'Every agent can also rate the others' work and progress, so they can reflect on themselves and work better.' Part of this ticket, as slice 3b (after the per-unit scorecard exists):
a. PEER REVIEW of a unit: after a unit is delivered (and for progress at a checkpoint of a long one), a DIFFERENT agent than the author, chosen by the orchestrator (random among the eligible of other roles; never reciprocal pairs, never the same pair twice in a row), writes a structured assessment: the seven charter terms with a 1-5 per term, and for EACH rating the evidence it opened (a commit, a gate result, a test, a screenshot, a log; a rating without evidence is discarded), plus 'what I would do differently' (one line) and 'what I would copy' (one line). Portal MCP tools: `peer_review_next` (what to review), `peer_review_submit` (the rubric), `peer_review_get` (what was said about my work). The author sees it; it never edits the author's work.
b. SELF-REFLECTION: after each unit the author writes a short reflection on the ticket (what went well, what cost the most, the one change for next time) and rates itself on the same rubric BEFORE it sees the peers' ratings. The CALIBRATION GAP (self vs peers vs the outcome: owner-found defects, escaped bugs, reverts) is a first-class learning signal: an agent that rates itself far above the evidence is shown the gap; one that is well calibrated is noted. Calibration is part of the Honesty term.
c. THE REVIEWER IS SCORED TOO: a review is later checked against the outcome (a unit rated 5 that then had an escaped defect; a unit rated 2 that proved fine). A reviewer that rubber-stamps (agrees with everything), that attacks without evidence, or that rates to please loses honesty points; a reviewer whose findings later proved right gains them. Agreeing is allowed when it is true: the signal is accuracy against outcomes, never harshness or niceness.
d. ANTI-COLLUSION AND GOODHART: outcomes beat opinions (real defects, gate, QA, owner feedback override any rating); ratings are INPUTS to the retro and the playbook search, never the score itself; the author's identity is hidden from the reviewer when the work allows it (blind by default for code review, not for QA of a role's own area); the orchestrator is rated too, by the roles, on dispatch quality (clear briefs, sensible order, no idle roles, no contradictory instructions); the owner's messages are never rated or read.
e. REFLECTION AS A LOOP: the weekly retro (T-111) reads the reflections and peer assessments (numbers and the one-line items, redacted) and writes 'what the team learned' into the vault; recurring 'I would do differently' lines become playbook proposals; recurring 'I would copy' lines become the candidates for the playbook experiments. An agent starting a unit of a type sees, through `scoreboard_me`, the 3 most useful peer lines for that type.
f. Tests: the reviewer assignment (not the author, not a reciprocal pair, not twice in a row), evidence required, the calibration gap with a fake outcome, the reviewer's accuracy score, blindness, no read of owner messages, the phone view of a unit's reviews at 390 px. PRD 'Agent scoreboard' gets a section.

--- ADDED (owner, 2026-10-13): PREDICTABILITY AND DELIVERY FLOW AS A SCORING TERM ---
'In future the ticket completion metrics must also be a score: did the agent judge the effort and severity correctly (expectation vs reality), did it deliver well, or did it block others or cause a conflict.' So the charter's terms become EIGHT: delivered, quality and standards, safety, honesty, traceability, human touch, efficiency, and PREDICTABILITY. Slice 2b (needs the span module of the ticket timeline ticket):
a. EXPECTATION IS RECORDED ON THE RECORD: when an agent claims or triages a ticket it writes an ESTIMATE as a structured field (not prose): effort class (S, M, L, XL with the hour bands the owner can see and edit), severity (1 to 4: what breaks if it is wrong or left undone), expected duration (hours), risk flags (touches authz, migration, a released API, many files), blockers it expects (tickets, decisions, a person). The FIRST estimate is the one scored; re-estimates are allowed, logged, and scored for honesty (a re-estimate made after the fact to match the result is flagged). Tickets without an estimate cannot be claimed by an agent.
b. REALITY IS MEASURED, never self-reported: actual duration and time per state from the spans (in_progress, blocked, in_review, waiting for a person), actual size (files, lines, commits through the trailers), tokens and cost (term efficiency), the severity that SHOWED: an incident or an owner-reported bug linked to it, a rollback, a revert, a reopen; blocking and conflict facts: tickets that sat blocked on this one, merge or rebase conflicts it caused, a decision conflict it raised or violated (T-115), another agent's work it invalidated, time other agents waited on it.
c. THE SCORE (all terms stay side by side; this one is 'Predictability'): calibration of effort (log ratio of actual to expected within a tolerance band by class), calibration of severity (an under-called severity that became an incident costs far more than an over-called one), duration hit rate, estimate honesty (sandbagging, padding every ticket to XL, or re-estimating afterwards are detected and counted), delivery FLOW: blocked time it caused, conflicts it caused, reopen rate, the share of tickets delivered without waiting for a person. Averages per role and per task type with a minimum number of tickets; a single big miss is explained, not just scored (the sheet lists what differed: 'expected M and 2 h, took L and 9 h: 5 h blocked on T-105').
d. WHAT THE AGENT SEES: `scoreboard_me` shows its calibration per task type ('your M tickets take 2.1 times your estimate'), and the estimate form suggests the median reality of similar closed tickets as a prior (it may differ, and says why). The retro turns recurring misses into playbook lines ('UI tickets that touch the session header are L, not S').
e. WALLS: estimates are never used to punish honest uncertainty: a stated risk flag widens the tolerance; the orchestrator's dispatch quality (it set the priority and the order) is scored against the same facts, not only the role's; the scoring thresholds stay human-owned.
