# T-129 Spikes: audit and calibrate for autonomous work, cause and unit on every event, in-flight detection, a clear view, and a scoring input

Owner (2026-10-13): 'improve the UI/UX and audit the spikes, and consider spikes as part of the scoring matrix too.' Read docs/prd/claude-usage.md section 24 (spike detection), apps/server/src/usagespend/spikes.ts, apps/web/src/features/usage/spend/SpikesTab.tsx (110 lines: a flat list of events, evidence in an InfoTip, a snooze menu), the SpikeEvent contract (packages/contracts/src/usagebudget.ts), T-122 (the usage dashboard, where Spikes becomes part of Alerts), T-111 and T-114 (the scoring).

AUDIT OF THE LIVE DATA (coordinator, 2026-10-13 15:42 UTC, GET /api/usage-spend/spikes?range=7d): THREE events in seven days, all of kind `outlier` (one request at least 10 times the median request of its Claude): fadlilah $22.34 (55 requests, 16.1 x a median of $1.39), hasyim $23.99 (36 requests, 12.1 x $1.98), fadlilah $15.14 (21 requests, 10.8 x $1.40). All three are an ORCHESTRATOR's long turn with sub-agents, i.e. normal work, not an anomaly. Meanwhile TODAY cost $501.94 over 215 requests and 59 agents, with 956 million cache-read tokens against 4.2 million output tokens, and NO rule fired on that. Findings: (1) the outlier rule compares unlike with like: a turn of an autonomous orchestrator is judged against the median turn of a Claude that mostly runs small turns; every big legitimate turn becomes noise and the alert loses meaning; (2) the real cost problem (a persistently high burn, a huge cache-read share, 59 agents, duplicated or repeated work) is not a spike and is invisible to these rules; (3) the detector sees a request only when it ends (PRD 24.4): a runaway inside one turn is found after it cost the money; (4) an event has no CAUSE and no UNIT: it names a session and a Claude but not the ticket, the role, the sub-agents or the tools that spent it, and the notification text says 'kohi-event outlier est.' (T-126 fixes the text); (5) a spike cannot be classified (expected, productive, wasteful) or taught ('this is normal for this role'); (6) the tab is a flat list: no time axis, no cost shown as a share of the day, no grouping, no next step.

BUILD, in this order (each merged on its own; measure before and after):
1. CALIBRATE ON HISTORY first. A replay harness (scripts or a test tool, scratch only) that runs the stored requests of the last 14 days through the current rules and through the new ones and prints, per rule, the events, their cost, their cause class and how many a person would have wanted. Labelled by the owner or the QA role on a sample of 20 events in one pass (the only human input: one tap per event 'expected / wasteful / unsure'); the thresholds are set from those labels, not guessed. Report on this ticket: before and after counts and the labelled precision.
2. BASELINES BY KIND OF WORK, not per Claude: compare a turn with the median of turns of the same CHAT KIND (an orchestrator turn with sub-agents, a worker turn, an interactive turn, a CI-started turn) and the same model; the outlier factor and the minimum dollars are settings per kind; a turn that includes sub-agents is compared by its cost per delivered unit as well (T-111).
3. NEW RULES, each a heuristic that says what it saw and never acts (the PRD's principle stays): SUSTAINED BURN (a Claude, chat or program above its budget pace for N hours: ties to the budgets and the pace lines of T-122); CONTEXT BLOAT (cache-read tokens per request growing past a ratio: the chat should checkpoint and restart from its status file); IDLE BURN (cost in turns that produced no commit, comment, run or file change); RETRY STORM (the same tool call failing three times, a test run repeated unchanged); MODEL MISMATCH (a top-tier model on a narrow sweep that a cheaper tier does as well, once the scoreboard has the numbers); AGENT FAN-OUT (more sub-agents running than the WIP limit asks); DUPLICATE WORK (two chats editing the same files). Each with its own cool-down and evidence.
4. IN-FLIGHT DETECTION: the stream already carries usage per assistant message: evaluate the running turn's cost and request count while it runs (cheap counters per chat, no database write per message), raise the alert at the threshold, and for autonomous chats send ONE steer message ('this turn has cost $X in N minutes: stop repeating, checkpoint, report') through the same priority path as the quota steer of T-105; never kill a turn.
5. ATTRIBUTION AND CAUSE: every event carries unit and cause: the session, the ticket (from the chat's ticket or the commit trailers), the role or sub-agent label, the model, the top contributors (tools, sub-agents, models) as numbers, the share of the day's spend, and a CAUSE CLASS: expected (the ticket's estimate said L or XL and the cost is within it), productive-unexpected (a change was delivered), wasteful (loop, retry storm, idle burn, rework), unknown. One tap on an event: 'This is normal for <kind of work>' teaches that kind's baseline (audited, reversible, and it widens that kind's threshold only), 'Wasteful' opens a refinement ticket for the unit.
6. UI/UX of the Spikes view (inside T-122's Alerts; phone first at 390 px, then 1280; the design standard T-120; the chart kit T-121): (a) a TIMELINE strip on top: time on x, each event a bubble sized by cost and coloured by cause class (with a pattern or letter: colour is never the only signal), the day's total spend as a faint area behind it, the same scales as the pace lines; tap a bubble for its sheet; (b) below it, events GROUPED by unit (ticket or chat) and by cause with a plain sentence each ('Orchestrator turn cost $22 (16 x its usual $1.40): 55 requests over 41 min on T-107'), the cause chip, the share of the day, and the next steps as buttons ('Open the chat at that turn', 'See what it spent on', 'Mark normal', 'Snooze', 'Set a per-turn budget'); (c) a SUMMARY on top: spike cost against total cost in the window, spikes by cause, a trend against the previous period, and 'wasteful spend' as the number to drive down; (d) the sheet shows the evidence as a small breakdown chart (tools, sub-agents, models) and the numbers with their basis (estimate or measured), never prose only; (e) sensitivity controls PER KIND OF WORK in a compact table with the current thresholds, defaults explained in an InfoTip, and a 'preview' that says how many events the last 14 days would have raised at the chosen values; (f) an honest empty state ('Nothing unusual in 7 days: watching <rules>, thresholds ...'), loading skeletons, error with Retry, 'unavailable' where a rule could not run; (g) 44 px targets, keyboard path, a data table alternative, CSV export, no sideways scroll, lists that hold still when events arrive.
7. SPIKES IN THE SCORING MATRIX (T-111 efficiency and predictability, T-114 scoreboard; the eight terms stay side by side): a per-unit and per-role SPIKE profile: wasteful spend as a share of the unit's cost (the number that counts; EXPECTED and PRODUCTIVE spikes are not penalised), wasteful events per $100, cost against the estimate (a ticket estimated M that cost XL, with the spike that explains it), time to react (how fast the chat stopped a loop after the steer), repeat offenders by kind of cause. It feeds: EFFICIENCY (wasteful share, retries, idle burn, context bloat), PREDICTABILITY (cost and duration against the estimate, spikes beyond it), HONESTY (a wasteful spike the agent did not mention in its report or reflection, a cause it labelled wrongly), SAFETY (a runaway that exhausts a budget is an incident under the charter), and the retro turns recurring causes into playbook proposals ('UI tickets that run the whole suite per edit cost 4 x: run the touched files'). Peer review (T-114) asks 'was this spike justified' with evidence. ANTI-GAMING: cost is attributed to the DELIVERED unit, not to the turn (splitting a task into small turns to stay under thresholds changes nothing), the baselines are per kind of work and audited when widened, an unknown cost is 'unavailable' not zero, and the owner's messages are never read by this scoring.
8. PERFORMANCE AND QUALITY (hard, as T-119/T-121/T-122): the spikes API p95 under 40 ms and under 30 KB gzipped; the in-flight counters add under 0.2 ms per message and never block the event loop; the rules run incrementally and are bounded; EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN asserted; the replay harness is a test fixture; every new rule has tests with real-shaped fixtures built at runtime (a loop, a retry storm, an idle burn, a context-bloat curve, a sub-agent fan-out) and a negative twin (a big legitimate turn that must NOT fire); the UI harness at 390 and 1280 px, both themes, screenshots you LOOK at; PRD claude-usage.md section 24 rewritten (observed, with the audit numbers above as the starting point).
