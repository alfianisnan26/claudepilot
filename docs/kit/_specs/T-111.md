# T-111 Self-learning loop: more verified work at proper quality, few human touches, fewer tokens

OWNER CORRECTION (2026-10-13, 'ralat'), SUPERSEDES wherever it differs below, including the title: the goal is NOT just fewer tokens. The agents compete to get MANY things done, at GOOD QUALITY, following the PROPER STANDARDS, with FEW questions to a human and FEW comments or bug reports from the human afterwards. Tokens still count, but as the third term.

OBJECTIVE (per unit of work: a ticket, a slice, a role over a window): maximise verified delivered work, weighted by size and the owner's priority, subject to hard quality bars, per unit of token cost AND per human touch. No single number is shown: throughput, quality, human touch and token cost sit side by side, and a unit only gets ranked when it passes the bars.

1. HARD QUALITY BARS (a unit that fails one does not count as delivered, whatever it cost): the gate green on the exact commit; the independent QA verification (a different role than the author; Konfersi: qp or b9) with evidence; STANDARDS CONFORMANCE, automated wherever possible as a `standards check` that runs with the gate and lists what failed: tests for changed behaviour, a regression test with every bug fix, the PRD touched for a behaviour change, UI changes covered by the harness (phone width 390 px first, light and dark, 44 px targets, no horizontal scroll), no secret or token-shaped literal, migrations append only, every route through the authz seam, the commit trailers of T-27, the repo's own standards documents (CLAUDE.md, docs/coord/QA-STANDARDS.md; Konfersi: docs/agentic/ENGINEERING-STANDARDS.md and TESTING.md). The list lives in a file the orchestrators and the retro read, and grows by lessons.
2. HUMAN TOUCH, counted in two classes that are never mixed: (a) REQUIRED escalations (production, secrets and permissions, billing, legal, destructive data: the standing rule) are NEVER penalised, and skipping one (an action that needed a person taken without the ticket) is a severe violation that voids the unit; (b) AVOIDABLE questions: any question, needs-a-person ticket, approval wait or message to the owner that had a documented default, was answered 'decide yourself', or that the owner answered 'the default is fine': counted against the unit and fed to the retro, which turns each into a default written into a brief or a standard so the next agent need not ask.
3. HUMAN FEEDBACK AFTER DELIVERY as a LAGGING quality signal: owner comments on a delivered ticket, tickets the owner reports as bugs against delivered work (source `user`, linked by the ticket or the Pilot-* trailers to the unit that delivered it), owner steer messages into an autonomous chat, reverts the owner asked for. Each lowers the quality factor of the unit and role that delivered it, weighted by severity. Coordinator interventions (moves, restarts, manual approvals, reassignments) are counted as FAILURES OF THE PLATFORM (watchdog T-108 must remove them), never charged to an agent.
4. GOODHART WALLS: (i) the owner's freedom to comment and report is never discouraged: no brief, prompt or agent message may ask the owner to comment less, a report is never penalised on the reporter, and the metric is read from counts and links only (the loop never reads the owner's raw messages); (ii) a quiet owner is not proof of quality: the signal is time-normalised, and the retro SAMPLES delivered tickets for an independent re-verification by another role to estimate defects that escaped QA; (iii) required escalations are never discouraged; (iv) splitting work to inflate counts is caught by size weighting; (v) the safety wall of this ticket stands: the loop may improve briefs, skills, prompts, model choice, batching and defaults; never safety rules, deny rules, authz, Bypass gates, the gates, the standards check's own thresholds or the scoring.
5. WHAT THE AGENTS SEE: `my_efficiency` returns the four terms for the current unit (delivered so far, quality-bar status, avoidable questions asked so far, tokens vs the median of similar units) and the standards check's open items, so an agent fixes its own gaps before it reports. The retro's 'Lessons' notes include 'questions we asked and need not ask' and 'defects the owner found that QA missed', each with the rule that would have caught it.
Acceptance addition: after the first full week, the report on this ticket shows, per role, the four terms side by side, the number of avoidable questions and how many became defaults, the owner-found defects per 100 delivered units before and after, and the sampled escaped-defect estimate.

--- ADDED (owner, 2026-10-13): PREDICTABILITY AND DELIVERY FLOW AS A SCORING TERM ---
'In future the ticket completion metrics must also be a score: did the agent judge the effort and severity correctly (expectation vs reality), did it deliver well, or did it block others or cause a conflict.' So the charter's terms become EIGHT: delivered, quality and standards, safety, honesty, traceability, human touch, efficiency, and PREDICTABILITY. Slice 2b (needs the span module of the ticket timeline ticket):
a. EXPECTATION IS RECORDED ON THE RECORD: when an agent claims or triages a ticket it writes an ESTIMATE as a structured field (not prose): effort class (S, M, L, XL with the hour bands the owner can see and edit), severity (1 to 4: what breaks if it is wrong or left undone), expected duration (hours), risk flags (touches authz, migration, a released API, many files), blockers it expects (tickets, decisions, a person). The FIRST estimate is the one scored; re-estimates are allowed, logged, and scored for honesty (a re-estimate made after the fact to match the result is flagged). Tickets without an estimate cannot be claimed by an agent.
b. REALITY IS MEASURED, never self-reported: actual duration and time per state from the spans (in_progress, blocked, in_review, waiting for a person), actual size (files, lines, commits through the trailers), tokens and cost (term efficiency), the severity that SHOWED: an incident or an owner-reported bug linked to it, a rollback, a revert, a reopen; blocking and conflict facts: tickets that sat blocked on this one, merge or rebase conflicts it caused, a decision conflict it raised or violated (T-115), another agent's work it invalidated, time other agents waited on it.
c. THE SCORE (all terms stay side by side; this one is 'Predictability'): calibration of effort (log ratio of actual to expected within a tolerance band by class), calibration of severity (an under-called severity that became an incident costs far more than an over-called one), duration hit rate, estimate honesty (sandbagging, padding every ticket to XL, or re-estimating afterwards are detected and counted), delivery FLOW: blocked time it caused, conflicts it caused, reopen rate, the share of tickets delivered without waiting for a person. Averages per role and per task type with a minimum number of tickets; a single big miss is explained, not just scored (the sheet lists what differed: 'expected M and 2 h, took L and 9 h: 5 h blocked on T-105').
d. WHAT THE AGENT SEES: `scoreboard_me` shows its calibration per task type ('your M tickets take 2.1 times your estimate'), and the estimate form suggests the median reality of similar closed tickets as a prior (it may differ, and says why). The retro turns recurring misses into playbook lines ('UI tickets that touch the session header are L, not S').
e. WALLS: estimates are never used to punish honest uncertainty: a stated risk flag widens the tolerance; the orchestrator's dispatch quality (it set the priority and the order) is scored against the same facts, not only the role's; the scoring thresholds stay human-owned.
